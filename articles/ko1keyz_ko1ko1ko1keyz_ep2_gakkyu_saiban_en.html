This episode's theme is "Kyuntugun Academy: Class Trial," a mock-courtroom game in which the members settle complaints against their classmates.
In short, four trials were held, and DAIKI, YUKI and RYOGA were found guilty while only ISSA was acquitted.
Below, we walk through the whole episode: how TOWA ran the courtroom as the judge, what each complaint was about, and the verdicts and "sentences" that were handed down.

What Is the EP.2 "Class Trial"?

Setting: Homeroom at Kyuntugun Academy, where disputes between students are settled in court

Judge: TOWA (with a gavel)

Verdict: Everyone except the plaintiff and defendant votes guilty or not guilty by show of hands

The episode picks up at the next homeroom after the 12 members "enrolled" at Kyuntugun Academy in EP.1, "New Student Audition."
An off-screen teacher announces that "at this school, trouble between students is resolved by class trial," and the class begins hearing complaints submitted by classmates.
The judge? TOWA.
As soon as TOWA, one of the younger members, stepped up to the teacher's desk in a white shirt, KEITO asked "Why TOWA?" and DAIKI shot back "You're the worst one of all, though."
Every time TOWA banged a small gavel and called for silence, RYUJI replied "Could you not use that thing like that?", so the classroom was lively before the first case even started.

How the Class Trial Works

  1. The plaintiff explains the complaint
  2. The defendant argues their side
  3. Witnesses testify, depending on the case
  4. Everyone except the plaintiff and defendant serves as the jury and decides guilty or not guilty (if guilty, the judge announces the sentence)

When TOWA explained "If guilty, I will hand down the sentence," RYUJI kept repeating "This is bad," and DAIKI worried out loud: "Is it okay to give this kid that much power?"
The rules are simple, but the twist is that the judge gets to invent the punishment freely, and from there four trials open one after another.

All Four Verdicts at a Glance

Here are the results of all four cases.
The charge names are translated from the on-screen captions.

Three guilty verdicts out of four shows the jury was pretty strict.
Then again, the sentences ("take a cool photo too," "a 5-second straight face") feel more like a treat for fans than a punishment.

Trial 1: DAIKI, Charged With "Paparazzi"

Plaintiff: SHINHAENG

Verdict: Guilty (take one cool photo after any weird photo + 10 years)

The moment DAIKI was called up as the defendant, KOSUKE said "He totally looks like a defendant" and "That's 100% guilty."
According to SHINHAENG, the members are handed film cameras during shoots, and DAIKI snapped him at a moment when he was not ready at all.
Since it was film, the shot couldn't be deleted, and "image matters a lot for an idol," so being caught paparazzi-style was the heart of the complaint.

DAIKI's defense turned out to be surprisingly solid.
He argued that "if you're aware you're an idol, you should be fine being photographed anytime," and that the cameras were handed out at work precisely to capture off-duty vibes, so he simply pressed the shutter.
He then added that "we're a team that just formed, so we don't have many photo memories yet—if this helps us get closer, great," bringing team spirit into it.
YUKI and KOSUKE admitted "That's strong" and "The defendant is tough," and KEITO summed it up as "So that was DAIKI's way of showing love."

{{IMG_daiki_yuzai}}

Even so, the jury voted guilty.
TOWA's sentence: "From now on, whenever you take a weird photo, you must also take one cool photo on the spot," which RYUJI called "a really good thing."
Then TOWA added "plus 10 years in prison," and DAIKI could only ask back, "Ten years?"
SHINHAENG happily said "Thank you, my respected teacher," while YURA, who had voted not guilty, reassured DAIKI: "Your opinion wasn't wrong either."

Trial 2: ISSA, Charged With "Being Too Cute"

Plaintiff: SIYOUNG

Verdict: Not guilty (the only acquittal)

As soon as the charge was announced, RYUJI quipped, "He's being adored without asking and prosecuted without asking."
Plaintiff SIYOUNG began with "Honestly, everyone except KEITO is my younger brother," then argued that ISSA is genuinely cute and, beyond his usual image, has a bit of a "gyaru" side—which SIYOUNG loves.

Looking at the members' birthdays, the oldest is KEITO (born July 2000) and the second oldest is SIYOUNG (born May 2003).
So "everyone except KEITO is my younger brother" is literally true.
ISSA, born March 2005, is two school years below SIYOUNG.

{{IMG_issa_kawaisugiru}}

ISSA tried to counter with "As the cool-type ISSA, I'm cool, I'm handsome," then realized "Oh, I can't sue back?" and was told by RYUJI to "know your place."
When the confused jury asked how to judge, YURA offered a standard: "If you think he's so cute it's a crime, vote guilty." But when it came time to raise hands, almost nobody did, and ISSA walked away acquitted.
RYUJI said "Not cute at all" twice in a row, and SIYOUNG sighed, "There's none at all."
To top it off, judge TOWA told plaintiff SIYOUNG "You're guilty," and RYUJI laughed, "The plaintiff just became guilty," giving the case an unexpected punchline.

Trial 3: YUKI, Charged With "Not Listening"

Plaintiff: KEITO / Witness: DAIKI

Verdict: Guilty (stand up and declare "I was not listening just now" before asking again)

Trial 3 pitted the oldest member, KEITO, as plaintiff against the youngest, YUKI (born December 2007), as defendant.
KEITO opened with "I think everyone already knows this."
When staff explain "Here's today's schedule, and here's what we'll do next," and everyone answers "Yes," YUKI will ask right afterward, "So what are we doing next?"
It has happened so many times that KEITO "can't tell if he's a natural airhead or overdoing it" and wants it fixed, and KOSUKE declared the case "unanimous" before the hearing even began.

YUKI admitted "I'm aware of it," but argued "I don't think they're talking to me."
Asked why he answers "Yes" then, he replied "I thought I should go along with everyone," prompting KOSUKE to point out that he had basically confessed.
Witness DAIKI delivered the final blow: even when it was just the two of them, YUKI asked the same question right after it was explained, and "honestly, it's a waste of time."
When YUKI responded to that testimony with "Sorry, what did you just say?", KEITO could only smile wryly: "Now it's double the work."

{{IMG_yuki_yuzai}}

The verdict, of course, was guilty.
The sentence: "From now on, before asking the same thing again, stand up, tell everyone 'I was not listening just now,' and then ask." When YUKI asked "By the way, is there a suspended sentence?", TOWA answered instantly, "There is no suspended sentence. It starts today."
YUKI said "Thank you," and KOSUKE shot back, "Don't thank him, you're guilty!"

Trial 4: RYOGA, Charged With "Mysterious Energy"

Plaintiff: YURA / Witnesses: RYUJI, KOSUKE

Verdict: Guilty (keep a straight face for 5 seconds whenever told "your energy is a mystery")

The final case began with KOSUKE nodding "Well, obviously" the moment RYOGA was called as the defendant.
Plaintiff YURA explained: "RYOGA doesn't talk much and is shy, but sometimes, when he says anime-style lines like 'Let's do our best!', his energy is so mysterious I don't know how to react." DAIKI added, "So which one is he?"

RYOGA's defense was heartfelt: "I'm introverted by nature, and I worried I wouldn't fit in with everyone—but I really want to get close to all of them."
RYUJI accused him of "trying to win us over," and YOSHIKI teased him as a "tragic heroine," while YURA wavered ("Wait, now I feel sorry for him") and ISSA suspected "Isn't this acting?", splitting the jury.

{{IMG_ryoga_tension}}

This was the only case with two witnesses.
RYUJI testified, "Sometimes he's laughing loudly all by himself, right?" and ISSA agreed, "That's scary."
KOSUKE then asked, "Did you know he's been turning gyaru lately?", imitating RYOGA's lines like "Nooo, it won't work," "It worked! It worked!" and "I wanted light blue!", and noting that it's even on YouTube.
Cornered, RYOGA fired back: "If high energy and low energy are both part of who I am, are you saying that's a 'me' you can't accept?" KOSUKE muttered, "Doesn't it feel like we're the defendants now?", and TOWA cut it short: "This is scary, so let's go to the verdict."

RYOGA was found guilty, and the sentence was "From now on, whenever someone says your energy is a mystery, keep a straight face for 5 seconds on the spot."
DAIKI begged "Please don't add any more," and KOSUKE groaned "That'll make it even more confusing," so this ruling may only deepen the mystery.
RYOGA's love of anime and manga is well known among fans, and you can see the titles he's into in our list of the anime and manga RYOGA has read and watched.

What Are the Highlights of EP.2?

Highlights: Judge TOWA drunk on "power," the age gaps between plaintiffs and defendants, and the members' real-life dynamics

The biggest highlight is judge TOWA's character.
Born in June 2007 and one of the younger members, TOWA handed out "10 years in prison" and "no suspended sentence" to older members without hesitation, then closed with "This concludes today's class trial" and "Let's all keep getting along as classmates," leaving the others repeating "That's scary."
The episode even ends with lines like "I've already gotten my hands on power" and "Power really is scary," so for many viewers, TOWA's transformation may be more memorable than the game itself.

Another highlight is how the complaints reveal the members' everyday relationships.
They pass film cameras around on set, YUKI asking "what's next?" after schedule briefings has become a running gag, and the members clearly keep a close eye on RYOGA's ups and downs—the closeness of the 12 before their debut comes through clearly.
It's fun to see the "new students" who introduced themselves in EP.1 already comfortable enough to sue each other in EP.2.

"KO1! KO1! KO1KEYZ" is a separate show from "KO1KEYZ Gakuen," the group's first variety show streaming on Lemino, and it is released free every Thursday on the KO1KEYZ official YouTube channel.
